analyses

The following are analyses that I've conducted while working at St. John's University. Each report seeks to answer a research question proposed by an academic or administrative department on campus.

Combined Degrees

In an effort to expand their combined degree programs, the Tobin College of Business wanted to be able to identify potential undergraduate students who had a high probability of being successful in (graduating from) a combined degree program. Specifically, they wanted to know what GPA cut-offs to use when extending offers to apply for their combined degrees. I used simple regression and ROC analysis to determine the optimal GPA cut-offs to use when extending offers, in addition to determining the cumulative credits necessary to make an informed decision.

Retention of Interdisciplinary Business Majors

The Tobin College of Business noticed that interdisciplinary business majors had a historically lower retention rate than other business majors, and wanted to understand what was driving this lower rate.

Adjusting for multiple known retention factors (gender, financial need, high school gpa) reduced the retention delta between IDB majors and all other business majors, but the difference was not completely erased. Ultimately, the results were inconclusive.

Changing Majors

Students applying to St. John's University who do not qualify for the major they applied for will be directed into a program with less rigorous admissions requirements. These admits are coded as "offered program" (OP) or "offered bachelors" (OB), depending on the situation. The Freshman Center wanted to understand the outcomes of these students; specifically, how many of them end up graduating from the majors that they were directed into. Additionally, they wanted to know about students who enroll as "undecided" or Liberal Studies majors (LST), make it to their second year without changing their major

The following is a descriptive analysis that seeks to answer these questions. I used a simple regression in one figure to demonstrate that high school gpa explains most of the variation in graduation rates.

Female Enrollment in the College of Business

The dean of the Tobin College of Business wanted to understand the gender make-up of the college over the past several years, and how the proportion of male:female students varied across different factors (commuters vs residents, early admits vs regular admits, etc.). He also wanted to understand how the gender proportions in the business school compared to other colleges at St. John's.

The report I provided was a descriptive analysis using 10 years of enrollment data. Using confidence intervals for regression coefficients, I identified enrollment trends as stable, increasing, or decreasing over the previous 10 years, and compared the business school trends to the university-wide trends for comparison.
